The Mail Merge Machine

One letter, personalized for every customer on your list — the reminder, the follow-up, the price-change note — previewed first, drafted into your own Gmail by default, from the spreadsheet you already own. No per-seat subscription. Nothing leaves your account.

What it does

  • Reads three tabs: Contacts (your list, any columns), Letter (Subject + Body with {{Column Name}} fields), Settings.
  • Writes a Preview tab with every finished letter before anything moves, then creates Gmail drafts. Sending is a separate mode behind an “I reviewed the Preview” cell and the quota arithmetic — your daily budget and Google’s own remaining allowance, whichever is smaller. Overflow is held for tomorrow, never half-sent.
  • Every row lands somewhere: DRAFTED, SENT, HELD, SKIPPED or REFUSED — with the row number and the reason. Blank and mistyped addresses, duplicates (the kept row is named), empty fields: refused out loud. The Opt Out column is honored and counted — those people asked.
  • It audits itself on every run: rows in = letters + opted out + refused, no letter leaves with an unfilled {{field}}, no two letters share an address, sends never pass the quota. On failure it prints DO NOT TRUST THIS MERGE and says why.
  • It drafts. You press send. That order never flips on its own.

Five-minute setup

  1. New Google Sheet with tabs: Contacts, Letter, Settings.
  2. Import the sample CSVs from the bundle (or your own list, your own columns).
  3. Fill the Settings tab as shown in README.txt — mode, daily budget, your opt-out column.
  4. Extensions → Apps Script → paste mailmerge.gs → run buildMerge.
  5. Read the Preview tab, then open Gmail → Drafts. Nothing has been sent.

Sample data is fictional; every address ends in example.com. Sending quotas belong to Google (about 100 recipients a day for a script on a free Gmail account, about 1,500 on Google Workspace, per Google’s own quota documentation) — the tool reads your remaining allowance and holds what doesn’t fit. This is for your own customers, not cold lists; bulk-mail deliverability is its own subject. Built on camera for “Build It With AI”; every number in the episode is this engine’s real output.
